### What I Learned
- The sUDT validation rule – better understanding
    - The previous week, I understood the rule as follows: sum(inputs) == sum(outputs). Today I figured out that it's not quite correct, actually sum(inputs) >= sum(outputs).
    - The difference in symbols allows the process of token burning when output tokens are less than input tokens. That's okay because you own your property, and you can destroy it. What's not allowed here is outputs > inputs – creating something out of nowhere.
- The two conditions for which the sUDT script exists
    - Condition #1 – Minting: When there are no input cells of this token type, the script returns 0 without even looking at the numbers. In this way, the minting process starts, and only once for one token type; further transactions have to follow the conservation principle.
    - Condition #2 – Transfer: When there are input cells of the token we are looking for, then the amount of input and output tokens is calculated. The conservation principle helps catch the moment when you try to create new tokens from nowhere.
 - The token change problem – most important lesson today
It’s a misconception that Ethereum developers have about CKB. In Ethereum, if you send 300 tokens to Bob, your balance will automatically go down from 1000 to 700 by itself.

In CKB, your cell with 1000 tokens is DESTROYED. Then you have to create 2 output cells on your own:
  - cell for Bob with 300 tokens
  - your own change cell with 700 tokens

If you fail to do it, you will lose your 700 tokens permanently. Your transaction won’t be noticed by the sUDT script as 1000 >= 300. The conservation rule prevents only CREATION, but not accidental burning.
