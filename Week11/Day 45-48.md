### What I learned
- Why I had to change from the file upload pattern to just adding the project's URL instead: My initial design had a drag-and-drop file upload mechanism.
However, after careful consideration, I scrapped that idea and adopted a URL submission mechanism. Why? First, storing the files directly on-chain incurs the cost of CKB usage,
based on the size of the files submitted. For example, a 10 MB movie would require
thousands of CKB to store its contents. This approach is impractical for the competitors.

Submitting a URL only involves submitting text - maybe 50-100 bytes - and costs practically nothing
nothing in CKB terms, while ensuring that the voters have access to the full content. 
Contestants submit the actual content they've created to YouTube, TikTok, Soundcloud, Instagram, 
or even GitHub, as they see fit, then submit the URL of the content.

This is actually the correct approach for many blockchain applications. The blockchain acts as proof of ownership. The content stays where it fits best. 

- The entry Cell structure: Every submission made by contestants creates a new cell on CKB 

- The submit flow follows the exact same 10-step pattern as that of the create contest:
    1. The form is first validated on submission
    2. Checks if the user's wallet is connected
    3. Picks up the creator's address
    4. Packages entry data as JSON
    5. Converts it to hex 
    6. Calculates capacity (61 + dtatBytes + 500 buffer)
    7. Builds the CKB transaction
    8. Signs and broadcast
    9. Saves TX hash to SQLite
    10. Redirects the creator back to the contest
